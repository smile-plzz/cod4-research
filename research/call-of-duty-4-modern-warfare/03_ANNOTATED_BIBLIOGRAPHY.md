## 1. Primary Empirical Objects & Developer Documentation

### P01. McCandlish, M., & Boon, M. (2008). The making of *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare*. *Game Developer*, 15(3), 32–40. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/the-making-of-call-of-duty-4-modern-warfare
- **Author Roles:** Mackey McCandlish served as Lead Designer and Michael Boon served as Technical Art Director at Infinity Ward during the development of *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare*.
- **Content Summary:** This authoritative postmortem offers an insider account of the game’s 24-month production cycle. The authors disclose that following *Call of Duty 2*, the team suffered severe creative fatigue regarding World War II and pledged never to return to the setting. The document details the "mid-production reboot" in 2006: after spending the first year building levels set exclusively in Middle Eastern desert environments against generic insurgent militias, the design team recognized that the game lacked geopolitical scope and dramatic variety. They halted production, cut several completed levels, and introduced the British SAS special-forces perspective, the Russian Ultranationalist civil war front, and the Chernobyl flashback sequence.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Indispensable primary historical evidence. Directly contradicts assumptions that the game's dual-theatre narrative was planned from inception, revealing it as an emergent design solution to combat monotony and mechanical repetition.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs Section 2 (Development History) and Section 10 (Creative Intentions), explaining the structural bifurcation between the SAS covert missions and the USMC invasion forces.

### P02. Shacknews Staff. (2007, June 13). *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare* developer interview. *Shacknews*. https://www.shacknews.com/article/47403/call-of-duty-4-modern
- **Author Roles:** Senior editorial staff interviewing Infinity Ward leadership (Vince Zampella, Jason West, Grant Collier).
- **Content Summary:** Pre-release interview detailing the design philosophy behind contemporary combat. The developers discuss hiring active-duty US Marine advisors and former British 22 SAS operatives to refine tactical reloading, room-clearing breach maneuvers, night-vision behavior, and weapon report audio. They discuss the strategic intent behind bullet penetration mechanics through wood, drywall, and sheet metal.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Validates developer intent regarding technical authenticity and mechanical tactility.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Supports Section 9 (Military Operations and Technology) and Section 11 (Realism and Authenticity).

### P03. Alavi, M. (2023, October 27). *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare’s* most iconic mission was a hard sell internally (Interview by D. Hornshaw). *GameSpot*. https://www.gamespot.com/articles/cod-4-modern-warfares-most-iconic-mission-was-a-hard-sell-internally-nobody-thought-it-was-cool/1100-6518706/
- **Author Roles:** Mohammad Alavi, Senior Level Designer and Scripter at Infinity Ward; creator of "All Ghillied Up," "One Shot, One Kill," and "No Russian."
- **Content Summary:** Alavi reveals that Infinity Ward’s leadership and colleagues initially opposed "All Ghillied Up," arguing that a slow-paced stealth mission where firing a weapon could cause instant failure ran counter to the franchise's identity. Alavi scripted the entire mission covertly, engineering custom AI logic that allowed enemies to pass within inches of a prone player in tall grass based on line-of-sight and movement velocity rather than proximity triggers.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Exceptional retrospective primary source clarifying the technical mechanics and procedural scripting required to construct the illusion of tactical patience and vulnerability.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Directly informs the focused analysis of *All Ghillied Up* in Section 8 and Section 12.

### P04. Burnett, R. (2016, October 4). *Call of Duty: Modern Warfare Remastered* — Interview with Ryan Burnett (Executive Producer, Raven Software). *Activision Games Blog*. https://blog.activision.com/it/call-of-duty/archives/call-of-duty-modern-warfare-remastered--interview-with-ryan-burnett-executive-producer-at-raven-software
- **Author Roles:** Executive Producer at Raven Software leading the remastering project.
- **Content Summary:** Burnett outlines the technical parameters established by Raven Software, Beenox, and Certain Affinity. The team’s primary directive was "gameplay parity": preserving the original 60 Hz tick-rate, player movement acceleration, sprint duration, jump heights, and weapon recoil curves while completely overhauling the visual rendering pipeline. Burnett details the implementation of physically based rendering (PBR), dynamic high-dynamic-range (HDR) lighting, re-recorded Foley sound effects, and rebuilt high-poly character models.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Core technical evidence documenting the boundary between mechanical preservation and aesthetic modernization.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Central to Section 14 (The 2016 Remaster: Technical Reconstruction and Narrative Preservation).

### P05. Pellas, D. (2016, September 3). How *Modern Warfare Remastered* updated the classic (Interview by B. Crecente / J. Gaudiosi). *Forbes* / *Polygon*. https://www.forbes.com/sites/erikkain/2016/09/03/everything-you-need-to-know-about-call-of-duty-modern-warfare-remastered/
- **Author Roles:** David Pellas, Studio Director at Raven Software.
- **Content Summary:** Pellas explains the studio's philosophy in treating *Call of Duty 4* as a cultural classic. He highlights how modernized visual clarity—such as volumetric smoke, realistic fire, and detailed facial animations—was intended to evoke how players *remembered* the game looking in 2007, rather than how it actually rendered on seventh-generation hardware. He also addresses the technical balance of the multiplayer maps.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Essential for examining memory, nostalgia, and remediation in digital game remasters.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Supports Section 14 and Section 15 (Discussion).

### P06. Infinity Ward. (2007). *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare* [Video game]. Activision.
- **Content Summary:** The original commercial release of the game on PC, Microsoft Xbox 360, and Sony PlayStation 3. Primary empirical text of this study, encompassing 21 campaign missions and multiplayer systems.

### P07. Raven Software. (2016). *Call of Duty: Modern Warfare Remastered* [Video game]. Activision.
- **Content Summary:** The updated technical release on PC, Sony PlayStation 4, and Microsoft Xbox One. Serves as the comparative empirical text for aesthetic, auditory, and systemic evaluation.

## 2. Foundational Theoretical Scholarship

### A01. Payne, M. T. (2016). *Playing war: Military video games after 9/11*. New York University Press. https://doi.org/10.18574/nyu/9781479805228.001.0001
- **Content Summary:** Payne investigates how post-9/11 commercial military games conditioned domestic players to accept the cultural logic of the Global War on Terror. He formulates the concept of "procedural citizenship," wherein players perform simulated military service and internalize the doctrine of pre-emptive intervention through gameplay routines. Payne devotes substantial analysis to *Call of Duty 4*, demonstrating how its distributed playable perspectives paradoxically reinforce a monolithic militarist ethos.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Premier foundational monograph in critical military game studies. Rigorous synthesis of cultural studies, political science, and ludic analysis.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs Section 3 (Literature Review), Section 4 (Theoretical Framework), Section 7 (Military Identity), and Section 10 (Geopolitical Representation).

### A02. Der Derian, J. (2009). *Virtuous war: Mapping the military-industrial-media-entertainment network* (2nd ed.). Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203875360
- **Content Summary:** Der Derian coins the concept of "virtuous war"—the contemporary military doctrine that seeks to wield clean, technologically sophisticated, distance-mediated lethal force with zero allied casualties, thereby insulating domestic publics from the visceral horrors of conflict. He maps the "MIMEN" (Military-Industrial-Media-Entertainment Network), illustrating how simulation technologies blur the lines between Pentagon training tools and civilian entertainment.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Foundational text in international relations and critical security studies.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Crucial for Section 10 (Geopolitical Analysis) and Section 12 (specifically the deconstruction of the AC-130 mission *Death From Above*).

### A03. Lenoir, T., & Caldwell, L. (2018). *The military-entertainment complex*. Harvard University Press. https://doi.org/10.4159/9780674984721
- **Content Summary:** Comprehensive historical investigation into the co-evolution of computing hardware, 3D graphics acceleration, and military funding from Cold War flight simulators to 21st-century console games. The authors examine how the entertainment software industry commercialized dual-use military simulation technologies.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Thorough historical and economic analysis grounded in archival documentation.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Provides political-economic contextualization for Section 3 and Section 15.

### A04. Robinson, N. (2012). Videogames, persuasion and the War on Terror: Escaping or embedding the military–entertainment complex? *Political Studies*, 60(3), 504–522. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9248.2011.00923.x
- **Content Summary:** Robinson examines whether modern military first-person shooters merely function as propaganda mechanisms for Western state security policies or whether they possess ambiguity that allows players to question state violence. Focusing closely on *Call of Duty 4*, he analyzes the nuclear blast sequence (*Shock and Awe* / *Aftermath*) and the remote gunship mission (*Death From Above*). Robinson concludes that while *Modern Warfare* introduces critical moments of military vulnerability, these moments are ultimately contained within a wider narrative structure that affirms the necessity of Western intervention.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Highly cited, methodologically rigorous political science investigation with balanced textual analysis.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Core framework for Section 3, Section 10, Section 12, and the Devil’s Advocate counterarguments.

### A05. Robinson, N. (2019). Military videogames: More than a game. *The RUSI Journal*, 164(4), 10–21. https://doi.org/10.1080/03071847.2019.1659607
- **Content Summary:** Published by the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI), this article assesses how contemporary armed forces engage with commercial military video games for public outreach, informal tactical socialization, and veteran cultural identity.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Authoritative defense-studies perspective directly bridging academic cultural studies and professional military institutions.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Supports Section 9 (Military Operations and Perspectives).

### A06. Cao, K. D. (2024). Military violence in video games: Defining and representing the real in Call of Duty 4 Modern Warfare. *Journal of Integrated Studies*, 15(1), 1–14. https://jis.athabascau.ca/index.php/jis/article/view/422
- **Content Summary:** Cao applies Roland Barthes’ literary concept of the "Reality Effect" (*l'effet de réel*) to *Call of Duty 4*. He demonstrates that the game's sense of authenticity is not generated by accurate geopolitical fidelity or historical truth, but by an accumulation of superfluous, highly specific technical signifiers: weapon model scratches, shell casing physics, tactical radio shorthand, and authentic military optics. These realistic micro-details persuade the player to accept a fabricated, ideologically loaded narrative of military necessity.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Rigorous semiotic and ludological case study directly addressing the core research question.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Forms the primary conceptual backbone for Section 11 (Realism, Authenticity, and the Reality Effect).

### A07. Parlak, C., & Kaygusuz, E. (2025). Neocolonialism in video games: The social discourse of Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare (2007). In *Conference Proceedings of DiGRA 2025: Games at the Crossroads*. Digital Games Research Association. https://doi.org/10.26503/dl.v2025i2.2480
- **Content Summary:** Applying Oliver Pérez Latorre’s framework for video game discourse analysis, the authors deconstruct the neocolonial structures embedded in *Call of Duty 4*. They analyze the spatialization of the game world—dividing it into civilized, orderly Western spaces and chaotic, dangerous Middle Eastern and Russian borderlands. They show how Western playable soldiers possess agency, moral clarity, and specialized expertise, whereas Middle Eastern characters (both combatants and the executed President Al-Fulani) are denied meaningful political subjectivity and framed as either fanatic insurgents or passive victims requiring Western rescue.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Timely, cutting-edge critical discourse analysis providing empirical textual proof of neocolonial rhetoric.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Central pillar for Section 10 (Political and Geopolitical Representation) and Section 15 (Discussion).

### A08. Van den Heede, P., Ribbens, K., & Jansz, J. (2018). Replaying today's wars? A study of the conceptualization of post-1989 conflict in digital "war" games. *International Journal of Politics, Culture, and Society*, 31(3), 229–250. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10767-017-9267-5
- **Content Summary:** The authors conduct an empirical content analysis of 106 commercial war games representing post-1989 conflicts. They find a systemic overrepresentation of Western special-forces operations, high-tech asymmetric interventions, and fictionalized rogue states, while structural political causes, socioeconomic contexts, and non-combatant civilian populations are almost universally erased.
- **Methodological Evaluation:** Robust quantitative and qualitative empirical survey establishing industry-wide baselines.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Establishes broader genre context in Section 3 and Section 9.

### A09. Stahl, R. (2010). *Militainment, Inc.: War, media, and popular culture*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203873298
- **Content Summary:** Stahl explores the cultural convergence of state militarism and commercial mass entertainment, conceptualizing "clean war" and the "interactive spectator." He argues that military shooters train citizens to view foreign policy through the optics of a gun-camera, transforming anti-war democratic deliberation into passive consumer spectatorship.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs Section 3, Section 10, and Section 12.

### A10. Pötzsch, H. (2015). The emergence of new "weapons of mass deception": Interactive simulation and the framing of contemporary war. *Nordicom Review*, 36(1), 35–48. https://doi.org/10.1515/nor-2015-0003
- **Content Summary:** Investigates how digital games function as framing devices that naturalize military intervention by establishing rigid, pre-programmed causal loops between external threats and kinetic response.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Theoretical support for Section 4 and Section 11.

### A11. Pötzsch, H. (2017). Selective realism: Filtering experiences of war and violence in first- and third-person shooters. *Games and Culture*, 12(2), 156–178. https://doi.org/10.1177/1555412015587802
- **Content Summary:** Develops the theory of "selective realism," demonstrating that military games achieve high realism in weapon mechanics, ballistics, and tactical movement while systematically filtering out traumatic collateral damage, civilian casualties, infrastructure ruin, and post-war psychological trauma.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Foundational conceptual tool for Section 11 and Section 15.

### A12. Gagnon, F. (2010). "Invading your living room": The ideology of first-person shooters and the post-9/11 War on Terror. *Intersections*, 11(1), 1–18.
- **Content Summary:** Examines how early post-9/11 FPS titles (including *Modern Warfare*) operationalized the Bush Doctrine of pre-emptive military strikes by framing foreign adversaries as imminent existential threats possessing weapons of mass destruction.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Direct historical-political context for Section 2 and Section 10.

### A13. Schulzke, M. (2013). The morality of playing war: Moral decision making in video games. *Ethics and Information Technology*, 15(2), 117–130. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-012-9304-4
- **Content Summary:** Analyzes the ethical frameworks built into military shooter mechanics, showing how friendly fire and non-combatant casualties are handled via instant mechanical fail-states ("Friendly fire will not be tolerated!"), thereby relieving the player of genuine moral deliberation by enforcing moral boundaries through system resets.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Supports procedural rhetoric analysis in Section 8 and Section 11.

### A14. Ramsay, D. (2015). Liminal spaces: Modern Warfare and the aesthetics of the post-9/11 military FPS. *Game Studies*, 15(2). http://gamestudies.org/1502/articles/ramsay
- **Content Summary:** Focuses specifically on the satellite map loading screens, radio broadcasts, and transition briefings of *Call of Duty 4*. Ramsay argues that these liminal, non-interactive sequences construct a continuous global geopolitical surveillance apparatus, connecting disparate missions into an urgent, totalizing military worldview.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Essential for Section 6 (Narrative Architecture) and Section 11 (Cinematic Storytelling).

### A15. Ramsay, D. (2020). *The geopolitical aesthetics of computer games*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780429447792
- **Content Summary:** Expands on spatial geopolitics in interactive media, analyzing how 3D game engines render foreign territories as target spaces and operational environments.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs Section 6, Section 9, and Section 10.

### A16. Huntemann, N. B., & Payne, M. T. (Eds.). (2010). *Joystick soldiers: The politics of play in military video games*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203884898
- **Content Summary:** Landmark anthology exploring the intersections of digital play, military ideology, and cultural reception across contemporary video games.
- **Contribution to Paper:** General theoretical underpinning for Sections 3, 9, and 13.

### A17. Höglund, J. (2008). Electronic empire: Orientalism revisited in the military shooter. *Game Studies*, 8(1). http://gamestudies.org/0801/articles/hoeglund
- **Content Summary:** Analyzes how contemporary first-person shooters remediate 19th-century colonial frontier myths and Edward Said's Orientalism, depicting the Middle East as an uncivilized, treacherous geography requiring imperial pacification.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Directly informs Section 10 (Political and Geopolitical Representation).

### A18. Šisler, V. (2008). Digital Arabs: Representation in video games. *European Journal of Cultural Studies*, 11(2), 203–220. https://doi.org/10.1177/1367549407088331
- **Content Summary:** Semi-quantitative and qualitative analysis of how Arab and Muslim characters are constructed in European and American commercial video games, showing their frequent reduction to undifferentiated, hostile targets.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Contextualizes character analysis of Al-Asad and enemy forces in Section 7 and Section 10.

### A19. Machin, D., & Suleiman, N. (2006). Arab computer games of the "War on Terror": An image of a new Middle East? *Patterns of Prejudice*, 40(4-5), 451–477. https://doi.org/10.1080/00313220601020102
- **Content Summary:** Explores Middle Eastern counter-narratives to Western military video games (such as *Special Force* and *Under Ash*), examining how visual representation and game mechanics reflect competing ideological claims during the War on Terror.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Cross-cultural context for Section 10.

### A20. Bos, D. (2018). The real and the unreal: Touring Chernobyl in virtual and digital spaces. *Area*, 50(4), 515–523. https://doi.org/10.1111/area.12423
- **Content Summary:** Explores dark tourism and spatial authenticity in digital depictions of the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone and Pripyat, focusing explicitly on *Call of Duty 4's* "All Ghillied Up." Bos argues that the game’s recreation of the abandoned Soviet landscape constructed a shared global visual memory of Pripyat.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Central to the Chernobyl case study in Section 8 and Section 12.

### A21. Smethurst, T. (2015). Playing with memory: The digital representation of nuclear disasters. *Memory Studies*, 8(3), 325–340. https://doi.org/10.1177/1750698014563870
- **Content Summary:** Investigates the representation of nuclear detonation, radiation fallout, and apocalyptic helplessness in video games, focusing on the nuclear shock in *Modern Warfare*.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Directly supports analysis of *Shock and Awe* and *Aftermath* in Section 8, Section 11, and Section 12.

### A22. Mirrlees, T. (2017). *Global entertainment media: Between cultural imperialism and cultural globalization*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315668383
- **Content Summary:** Analyzes the global political economy of digital entertainment, examining how defense contracting, security culture, and commercial software publishers align to project state power globally.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Context for Section 3 and Section 15.

### A23. Bogost, I. (2007). *Persuasive games: The expressive power of videogames*. MIT Press. https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/5334.001.0001
- **Content Summary:** Introduces the foundational theory of "procedural rhetoric"—how computational systems, rule structures, and interaction loops mount persuasive claims about how real-world systems function.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs procedural analysis in Sections 4, 8, 9, and 13.

### A24. Juul, J. (2005). *Half-real: Video games between real rules and fictional worlds*. MIT Press.
- **Content Summary:** Formulates the relationship between deterministic ludic rules and interpretive fictional worlds, providing the framework for analyzing ludonarrative resonance and dissonance.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Informs Section 4 and Section 13.

### A25. Barthes, R. (1986). The reality effect (*L'effet de réel*). In *The rustle of language* (R. Howard, Trans., pp. 141–148). Hill and Wang. (Original work published 1968).
- **Content Summary:** Semiotic treatise explaining how superfluous descriptive details that do not advance the plot function to announce: "we are the real."
- **Contribution to Paper:** Theoretical foundation for analyzing visual, acoustic, and tactical detail in Section 4 and Section 11.

### A26. Bolter, J. D., & Grusin, R. (1999). *Remediation: Understanding new media*. MIT Press.
- **Content Summary:** Establishes the concepts of "remediation," "immediacy," and "hypermediacy," showing how new digital media continuously absorb, translate, and reformat older media forms (such as television news broadcasts and helmet-cam footage).
- **Contribution to Paper:** Supports analysis of game cinematics and gun-camera displays in Section 11 and Section 12.

### A27. Newman, J. (2012). *Best before: Video games, supersession and preservation*. Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203144343
- **Content Summary:** Investigates the politics of video game remasters, platform updates, and cultural memory, demonstrating how remasters alter the historical text while claiming fidelity to player memory.
- **Contribution to Paper:** Foundational framework for Section 14 (The 2016 Remaster).
