# 06. Master Character Analysis & Psychological Profiling

This document delivers an exhaustive, multi-dimensional academic investigation of every major and secondary character in *Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare* (2007) and *Modern Warfare Remastered* (2016). Each major figure is evaluated across a standardized 16-dimension analytical matrix grounded in narrative theory, military studies, and game studies.

---

## 1. Sergeant John "Soap" MacTavish

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Sergeant John "Soap" MacTavish; British 22nd Special Air Service (SAS) Regiment, 22 SAS Bravo Team. Scottish nationality. |
| **Background** | Newly inducted operative selected for the elite 22 SAS after serving with distinction in the Parachute Regiment. Arrives at Credenhill training facility with exemplary marks in close-quarters battle (CQB) and demolition, but unproven in Tier-1 black operations. |
| **Introduction** | *F.N.G.* (Prologue): Introduced walking into the Credenhill hangar with a duffel bag, subjected to immediate hazing and evaluation by Captain Price and Gaz (*"Right. What the hell kind of name is 'Soap', eh? How'd a muppet like you pass selection?"*). |
| **Motivation** | Professional competence, unit integration, mission execution, loyalty to Captain Price and Bravo Team, and neutralization of the global nuclear threat. |
| **Personality** | Stoic, highly disciplined, observant, laconic, professional under extreme physical trauma, resilient. |
| **Ideology** | Pragmatic military professional; operates strictly within the institutional code of special-forces doctrine and state security. Lacks explicit political speeches, expressing ideology entirely through tactical obedience. |
| **Relationships** | **Captain Price:** Subordinate-to-mentor/father-figure dynamic. Evolves from initial skepticism to absolute mutual trust. **Gaz:** Immediate squad peer/instructor; bantering camaraderie. **Griggs:** Cross-national mutual respect formed under combat pressure. |
| **Character Development** | Transforms from an insecure, mocked recruit ("the F.N.G.") into a hardened Tier-1 combat veteran capable of preventing nuclear holocaust and executing the primary antagonist under mortal injury. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Exhibits profound psychological compartmentalization and stress inoculation. Experiences severe physiological shock during the Bering Strait sinking, the Azerbaijan retreat, and the bridge collapse, but maintains cognitive executive function. |
| **Military Function** | Point man, breacher, demolitions specialist, and assault operative for SAS Bravo Team. |
| **Narrative Function** | Primary focalizer for the SAS campaign; functions as the player's primary pedagogical vessel and tactical surrogate. |
| **Symbolic Function** | Embodies the archetype of professional British special-forces lethality: surgical, unglamorous, effective, and operating in the shadows. |
| **Player Relationship** | Complete first-person somatic embodiment. Silent protagonist whose silence allows the player to project their own cognitive state onto his hands and weapon manipulation. |
| **Major Scenes** | The killhouse evaluation (*F.N.G.*); catching Price's hand during the cargo ship sinking (*Crew Expendable*); uploading missile abort codes (*No Fighting in the War Room*); catching the M1911 and shooting Imran Zakhaev (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Survives the bridge massacre with severe internal injuries; extracted via Russian Loyalist helicopter while watching medics attempt to revive Captain Price. |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Soap demonstrates how modern military games construct player identity not through rich biographical exposition, but through the procedural acquisition of mechanical competence. |

## 2. Captain John Price (2011 Commander & 1996 Lieutenant)

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Captain John Price (callsign "Bravo Six"); Officer Commanding, 22 SAS Bravo Team. British Army. Previously Lieutenant under Captain MacMillan in 1996. |
| **Background** | Veteran of decades of clandestine operations across the Balkans, the Middle East, and the former Soviet sphere. Carries deep personal history with Imran Zakhaev dating back to the 1996 Pripyat assassination attempt. |
| **Introduction** | *F.N.G.*: Seen leaning over the catwalk with his iconic boonie hat and cigar, observing Soap's arrival with cynical detachment. |
| **Motivation** | Prevention of global catastrophe, containment of Russian Ultranationalism, protection of his men, and personal closure regarding his failure to kill Zakhaev in 1996. |
| **Personality** | Charismatic, cynical, fiercely loyal, ruthless when necessary, morally ambiguous in intelligence extraction, paternal toward his squad. |
| **Ideology** | Utilitarian realism. Believes international law and bureaucratic rules are luxuries sustained by men like him who do dirty work in the dark (*"We get our dirty hands so that the world stays clean"* ethos). |
| **Relationships** | **Soap:** Primary protégé and surrogate son. **Gaz:** Trusted second-in-command and operational right hand. **MacMillan:** Former mentor who shaped his tactical worldview. **Kamarov:** Uneasy proxy ally. **Zakhaev:** Lifelong nemesis. |
| **Character Development** | The 1996 flashback contextualizes his 2011 persona: as a young Lieutenant, Price was obedient, patient, and subordinate to MacMillan's meticulous instruction. By 2011, he has become the master commander who carries the burden of past failure. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Displays hyper-vigilance, emotional resilience, and deep-seated guilt over the geopolitical fallout caused by his incomplete assassination of Zakhaev. Exhibits decisive, cold calculation when executing Al-Asad. |
| **Military Function** | Operational commander, tactician, close-quarters combatant, sniper, and field interrogator. |
| **Narrative Function** | The emotional and moral anchor of the entire narrative; drives the plot forward through decisive field intelligence and leadership. |
| **Symbolic Function** | The quintessential post-imperial British soldier: an experienced warrior navigating the chaotic collapse of geopolitical order through personal authority rather than imperial might. |
| **Player Relationship** | Primary NPC commander in 2011; playable protagonist in the 1996 Chernobyl missions (*All Ghillied Up* / *One Shot, One Kill*). The shift from following Price to *becoming* young Price deepens player intimacy with his character. |
| **Major Scenes** | Rescuing Soap on the cargo ship ramp (*Crew Expendable*); executing Al-Asad after answering the satellite phone (*Safehouse*); taking the sniper shot on Zakhaev (*One Shot, One Kill*); sliding his pistol to Soap (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Critically wounded by Hind gunship fire on the bridge; unconscious and receiving desperate chest compressions from a Loyalist medic as the screen fades to black. |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Price represents the moral tension of virtuous war: an individual who commits extrajudicial executions and black operations presented as the ultimate protector of global civilization. |

## 3. Gaz

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Gaz (first name unrevealed; rank Sergeant/Staff Sergeant); 22 SAS Bravo Team. British Army. |
| **Background** | Highly experienced senior SAS non-commissioned officer, specialist in breaching, technical reconnaissance, and tactical electronics. Longstanding service alongside Captain Price. |
| **Introduction** | *F.N.G.*: Welcomes Soap to the Credenhill training range, explaining weapon handling and CQB mechanics with dry British wit. |
| **Motivation** | Squad survival, professional tactical execution, steadfast loyalty to Captain Price. |
| **Personality** | Sarcastic, sharp-witted, dry, highly competent, cynical regarding allied proxy forces (*"Price, Kamarov's asking for fire support... again"*). |
| **Ideology** | Tactical pragmatism. Views combat through the lens of tradecraft and technical efficiency rather than grand geopolitical rhetoric. |
| **Relationships** | **Captain Price:** Indispensable right-hand man; shares unspoken tactical understanding. **Soap:** Elder peer and technical coach. **Kamarov:** Openly contemptuous of Kamarov’s tactical delays. |
| **Character Development** | Maintains steady professional composure throughout the escalating global crisis, providing grounded humor until his shocking, sudden death strips the narrative of its comedic buffer. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Uses dry humor as an adaptive coping mechanism against extreme combat stress and operational absurdity. |
| **Military Function** | CQB point breacher, electronics/demolitions technician, second-in-command of Bravo Team. |
| **Narrative Function** | Serves as the primary voice of tactical exposition and squad camaraderie, humanizing the SAS unit. |
| **Symbolic Function** | The working-class professional soldier: unpretentious, technically brilliant, and entirely expendable in the machinery of state security. |
| **Player Relationship** | Constant companion NPC who guides the player through door breaches, rappel sequences, and obstacle clearing. |
| **Major Scenes** | Guiding Soap through the killhouse (*F.N.G.*); cutting power to the cottage (*Blackout*); assisting in Al-Asad’s interrogation (*Safehouse*); breach assault on the missile silo (*No Fighting in the War Room*); execution by Zakhaev (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Executed point-blank with a Desert Eagle by Imran Zakhaev on the collapsed highway bridge while lying wounded and helpless. |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Gaz's unceremonious execution delivers one of the game's most visceral affective shocks, demonstrating that mechanical competence and charismatic humor offer zero armor against lethal violence. |

## 4. Captain MacMillan

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Captain MacMillan (callsign "Baseplate"); Officer Commanding, 22 SAS black-ops sniper detachment in 1996. Scottish nationality. |
| **Background** | Elite British reconnaissance sniper operating during the immediate post-Cold War era. Selected by the British Ministry of Defence to execute the UK government’s first state-sanctioned assassination since the Second World War. |
| **Introduction** | *All Ghillied Up* (Act II Flashback): Seen emerging out of the tall grass in a full ghillie suit, whispering orders to Lieutenant Price (*"Too much radiation. We'll have to go around"*). |
| **Motivation** | Flawless execution of the Chernobyl black-ops mission, evasion of detection, preservation of operational stealth, extraction of his subordinate. |
| **Personality** | Patient, methodical, deeply observant, unflappable, stern yet supportive mentor (*"Good night, ya bastard"*, *"Patience, lad. Don't do anything rash"*). |
| **Ideology** | Absolute doctrine of silent tradecraft: combat avoidance is superior to engagement; kinetic force is a catastrophic failure of stealth. |
| **Relationships** | **Lieutenant John Price:** Mentor-to-protégé relationship. MacMillan molds Price's understanding of positioning, patience, and ballistics. |
| **Character Development** | Represents the calm apex of military tradecraft until his catastrophic leg injury in *One Shot, One Kill*, which reverses the power dynamic and forces him to rely entirely on young Price for survival. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Epitomizes hyper-controlled cognitive regulation; suppresses physical agony to direct tactical defense while immobilized at the Ferris wheel. |
| **Military Function** | Master sniper, spotter, deep reconnaissance specialist, field commander. |
| **Narrative Function** | Establishes the historical precedent for the 2011 crisis and provides the genetic blueprint for Price’s own later command style. |
| **Symbolic Function** | Represents an older, meticulous generation of Cold War special reconnaissance before the hyper-kinetic era of the Global War on Terror. |
| **Player Relationship** | Tactical mentor NPC whose movement cadence, prone pauses, and target selections dictate every input the player makes in *All Ghillied Up*. |
| **Major Scenes** | Guiding Price under BMP armored vehicles and infantry patrols (*All Ghillied Up*); acting as spotter for the Barrett .50 cal shot (*One Shot, One Kill*); leg crushed by a falling Mi-28 Havoc; carried by Price to the extraction zone. |
| **Final Outcome** | Successfully evacuated from Pripyat via Sea Knight helicopter; survives to retire and later consult for British intelligence (referenced in later lore). |
| **Critical Interpretation** | MacMillan's presence demonstrates how game mechanics (stealth, waiting, holding breath) can construct a masterclass in tension without relying on rapid violence. |

## 5. Sergeant Paul Jackson

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Sergeant Paul Jackson; 1st Reconnaissance Battalion, Bravo Company, United States Marine Corps (USMC). American nationality. |
| **Background** | Enlisted Marine non-commissioned officer deployed as part of the massive US amphibious intervention force sent to depose Khaled Al-Asad in the Middle East. |
| **Introduction** | *Charlie Don't Surf* (Act I): Sitting inside a CH-46 Sea Knight transport as it skims the ocean toward a Middle Eastern coastal city at dawn. |
| **Motivation** | Tactical execution of squad objectives, suppression of insurgent resistance, rescue of downed comrades, adherence to USMC chain of command. |
| **Personality** | Stoic, obedient, relentless, courageous in room-clearing and defensive stands. |
| **Ideology** | Conventional military devotion to the US Marine Corps ethos (Semper Fidelis) and American interventionist power. |
| **Relationships** | **Lieutenant Vasquez:** Direct commanding officer; absolute obedience. **Staff Sergeant Griggs:** Squad peer and heavy weapons support. **Captain Pelayo:** Downed Cobra pilot whose rescue Jackson undertakes at the cost of his life. |
| **Character Development** | Jackson experiences no traditional character arc of dialogue or personal growth. Instead, his development is purely corporeal: moving from the hyper-empowered spearhead of American military power to an incinerated, dying body in the nuclear ash. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Represents the psychology of the front-line infantryman: hyper-focused on immediate cover, bounding maneuvers, and squad survival, completely blind to the larger geopolitical trap being sprung around him. |
| **Military Function** | Reconnaissance rifleman, grenadier, forward air controller / laser-designator, heavy assault point man. |
| **Narrative Function** | Demonstrates the destructive hubris and tragic vulnerability of conventional American military intervention in the Middle East. |
| **Symbolic Function** | The tragic symbol of the disposable front-line soldier. He is the physical manifestation of imperial overreach: a soldier deployed on false assumptions who dies in an atomic wasteland. |
| **Player Relationship** | Complete first-person somatic embodiment. The player experiences immense agency through his weapons, only to have that agency completely stripped away in *Aftermath*. |
| **Major Scenes** | Breaching the TV broadcast station (*Charlie Don't Surf*); holding the perimeter around the immobilized tank (*The Bog*); pulling Captain Pelayo from the burning Cobra wreckage (*Shock and Awe*); dying of radiation poisoning (*Aftermath*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Killed by blast trauma and acute radiation sickness in the nuclear detonation in the Middle Eastern capital (*Aftermath*). |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Jackson’s death represents a defining moment in video game history: the deliberate mechanical subversion of the player's expectation of heroic survival. |

## 6. Lieutenant Vasquez

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | 1st Lieutenant Vasquez; Platoon Commander, 1st Reconnaissance Battalion, USMC. American nationality. |
| **Background** | Experienced US Marine officer leading front-line combat elements during urban assault operations in the Middle East. |
| **Introduction** | *Charlie Don't Surf*: Directs Jackson and the squad during the helicopter insertion, coordinating the breach of the television station. |
| **Motivation** | Aggressive mission accomplishment, protection of his Marines, destruction of Al-Asad's forces. |

| **Ideology** | Unapologetic belief in American military supremacy and combined-arms dominance. |
| **Relationships** | **Jackson:** Trusted squad lead whom he places at the tip of the spear. **Griggs:** Respected heavy weapons NCO. |
| **Character Development** | Exemplifies the confidence and momentum of the initial American invasion until his fateful, noble decision to divert the evacuation transport to rescue Captain Pelayo seals the entire unit's doom. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Driven by the core USMC tenet of "leave no one behind," creating a fatal psychological inability to retreat when a comrade is in danger. |
| **Military Function** | Tactical unit commander, communications relay to Central Command, close-quarters squad leader. |
| **Narrative Function** | Represents conventional American military authority and command ethos. |
| **Symbolic Function** | The embodiment of American military interventionism: bravura, tactical competence, and honorable loyalty culminating in catastrophic strategic destruction. |
| **Player Relationship** | Direct NPC superior whose vocal commands structure the pacing of the USMC levels. |
| **Major Scenes** | Breaching the TV studio (*Charlie Don't Surf*); night defense of the tank (*The Bog*); ordering the rescue of Pelayo (*Shock and Awe*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Killed instantly when the nuclear shockwave destroys the CH-46 Sea Knight transport (*Shock and Awe*). |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Vasquez illustrates how tactical virtue (rescuing a downed pilot) can lead directly to strategic annihilation when confronting weapons of mass destruction. |

## 7. Staff Sergeant Griggs

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Staff Sergeant Griggs; 1st Reconnaissance Battalion, USMC; later attached to 22 SAS Bravo Team. African American nationality. Modeled on and voiced by Infinity Ward audio lead Mark Grigsby. |
| **Background** | Veteran USMC heavy machine gunner and squad leader. Survives the Middle Eastern operations by missing the fatal final helicopter lift in *Shock and Awe*, subsequently volunteering for the joint Anglo-American task force in Russia. |
| **Introduction** | *Charlie Don't Surf*: Sits across from Jackson in the Sea Knight, nodding to hip-hop beats in his headphones and checking his M249 SAW. |
| **Motivation** | Combat support, brotherhood, survival of his allies, tracking down those responsible for the nuclear deaths of his Marine brothers. |
| **Personality** | Outgoing, charismatic, culturally expressive, intensely loyal, humorous under fire, exceptionally brave. |
| **Ideology** | Devotion to his fellow soldiers; driven by deep personal loyalty rather than abstract ideology. |
| **Relationships** | **Jackson & Vasquez:** Close USMC squad comrades. **Soap & Price:** Initially viewed with cultural curiosity; quickly earns their unconditional respect through raw combat bravery. |
| **Character Development** | Serves as the vital narrative and emotional bridge between the obliterated USMC campaign and the SAS pursuit of Zakhaev. His willingness to operate alongside the British special forces unifies the two operational threads. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Channels survivor’s guilt from the nuclear catastrophe into aggressive, protective combat energy. |
| **Military Function** | Heavy machine gunner (M249 SAW), squad automatic weapon specialist, joint-task-force operator. |
| **Narrative Function** | Connects the two halves of the campaign; injects warmth and expressive humanity into the otherwise austere special-forces narrative. |
| **Symbolic Function** | Embodies the enduring bond of military brotherhood across national branches; the American who stands shoulder-to-shoulder with his British allies to the end. |
| **Player Relationship** | Beloved companion NPC who provides suppressing fire and saves the player's life. |
| **Major Scenes** | Clearing the TV station (*Charlie Don't Surf*); rescued from capture in the Altay Mountains (*Ultimatum*); joint silo breach (*No Fighting in the War Room*); dragging the wounded Soap to safety before being shot (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Shot through the neck and killed by Ultranationalist forces on the bridge while physically pulling Soap out of the line of fire (*Game Over*). |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Griggs' death is a profound emotional sacrifice: he dies not while shooting, but while shielding a wounded comrade, representing the ultimate moral nobility of the soldier within a bleak geopolitical tragedy. |

| **Background** | Senior Russian military officer commanding Loyalist troops fighting the Ultranationalist insurgency in the Caucasus and Central Russia. Has prior operational history with Captain Price. |
| **Introduction** | *Blackout* (Act I): Met on a cliffside overlooking a burning village in the Caucasus, smoking a cigarette and observing Ultranationalist positions. |
| **Motivation** | Defeating the Ultranationalist insurrection, preserving the Russian government, securing Western military assets for his own front. |
| **Personality** | Pragmatic, transactional, stubborn, weary, opportunistic, yet fundamentally dependable in extreme crises. |
| **Ideology** | Russian constitutional loyalism; opposed to Ultranationalist fascism and Soviet revanchism. |
| **Relationships** | **Captain Price:** Longstanding professional connection characterized by tactical bartering and cynical banter (*"Price... my friend!"* / *"Kamarov, you're not my friend"*). |
| **Character Development** | Initially appears as an exasperating proxy ally who exploits the SAS for his own tactical gain, but redeems himself as the ultimate savior who arrives at the bridge to rescue Soap. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Hardened by internal civil conflict; accustomed to catastrophic national instability and transactional alliances. |
| **Military Function** | Loyalist field commander, forward observer, air-support coordinator. |
| **Narrative Function** | Illustrates the messy reality of proxy warfare and foreign military alliances. |
| **Symbolic Function** | Represents the divided soul of post-Soviet Russia: a regular soldier fighting a desperate internal battle against radical nationalist extremism. |
| **Player Relationship** | Ambivalent ally NPC who delays the player's mission in *Blackout*, but serves as the *deus ex machina* in *Game Over*. |
| **Major Scenes** | Stalling the Nikolai rescue to demand sniper support (*Blackout*); assisting in Al-Asad’s village assault (*Safehouse*); joint ambush of Viktor (*The Sins of the Father*); arriving with Mi-28 gunships to save Soap (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Survives the campaign; successfully extracts Soap and secures the missile crisis site. |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Kamarov complicates simple East-West binaries by presenting a Russian officer who is an indispensable, heroic partner to the Western protagonists. |

| **Final Outcome** | Survives; flies exfiltration helicopters and remains a key asset for Price. |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Nikolai underscores the dependency of kinetic military force upon human intelligence and clandestine espionage. |

## 10. Imran Zakhaev

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Imran Zakhaev; Supreme Commander of the Russian Ultranationalist Party. Russian nationality. |
| **Background** | Soviet military veteran and industrial oligarch who built an illegal arms trafficking empire in the chaos following the 1991 collapse of the Soviet Union. Survived an SAS sniper assassination attempt in Pripyat in 1996, losing his left arm. |
| **Introduction** | *The Coup*: Appears silently beside Khaled Al-Asad during the public broadcast execution of President Al-Fulani. Formally identified during the Azerbaijan safehouse revelation (*Safehouse*). |
| **Motivation** | Restoration of the Soviet sphere of influence, expulsion of Western cultural and political dominance, destruction of the Russian constitutional government, and vengeance against the West. |
| **Personality** | Cold, ruthless, fanatical, strategic, patriarchal, vengeful. |
| **Ideology** | Russian Ultranationalism: a toxic amalgamation of Soviet nostalgia, imperial restoration, Slavic ethno-nationalism, and violent anti-Western revanchism. |
| **Relationships** | **Viktor Zakhaev:** Beloved son and heir; Viktor’s suicide shatters Imran's strategic composure. **Khaled Al-Asad:** Foreign proxy financed to distract the US military. **Captain Price:** Historical nemesis. |
| **Character Development** | Begins as a shadow puppeteer orchestrating global diversionary conflicts; after his son's suicide, he descends into suicidal, apocalyptic nihilism, launching ICBMs to destroy the American continent. |
| **Psychological Analysis** | Narcissistic geopolitical grievance magnified by severe trauma (the loss of his arm in 1996 and the suicide of his son in 2011). |
| **Military Function** | Supreme political and military commander, strategic financier, proxy war coordinator. |
| **Narrative Function** | The ultimate antagonist; the true architect whose presence links the Middle Eastern and Russian fronts into a unified global conspiracy. |
| **Symbolic Function** | The embodiment of Cold War revanchism and nuclear proliferation anxieties in the post-Soviet world. |
| **Player Relationship** | Distant target viewed through a sniper scope in 1996; executioner who stands over the player in 2011; final target shot in the chest by the player's pistol. |
| **Major Scenes** | Present at Al-Fulani's execution (*The Coup*); losing his arm in the 1996 sniper strike (*One Shot, One Kill*); launching nuclear ICBMs (*Ultimatum*); executing Gaz and standing over Soap on the bridge (*Game Over*). |
| **Final Outcome** | Shot three times in the chest and killed by Sergeant Soap MacTavish on the bridge using Price's M1911 pistol (*Game Over*). |
| **Critical Interpretation** | Zakhaev represents the Western fear of the un-pacified post-Soviet space: a heavily armed, ideologically fanatical enemy wielding nuclear weapons that render conventional Western military superiority moot. |

## 13. President Yasir Al-Fulani

| Dimension | Analytical Profile |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Yasir Al-Fulani; democratically elected President of an unnamed Middle Eastern nation. Middle Eastern nationality. |
| **Background** | Moderate, reformist, pro-Western political leader whose administration allied with Western powers, generating violent backlash from Al-Asad’s extremist movement. |
| **Introduction** | *The Coup*: Inhabited directly by the player from a first-person perspective in a bruised, disoriented state as he is dragged into a car. |
| **Motivation** | Survival, comprehension of the coup, desperate witnessing of his nation's collapse. |
