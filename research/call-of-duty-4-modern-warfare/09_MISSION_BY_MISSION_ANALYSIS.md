### MISSION 1: F.N.G.
- **Act:** Prologue
- **Timeline:** Day 1 — 07:00 (May 2011)
- **Location:** Credenhill, Herefordshire, United Kingdom (SAS Training Grounds)
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish (SAS)
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain John Price, Gaz, various SAS recruits
- **Mission Premise:** Soap's operational induction into 22 SAS Bravo Team; baseline CQB and marksmanship evaluation.
- **Story Progression:** Establishes Soap as a newly assigned recruit subjected to institutional hazing before immediate deployment.
- **Character Development:** Introduces Gaz as the sarcastic instructor and Captain Price as the demanding, cigar-chomping patriarch. Soap is silent, proving his identity entirely through physical execution.
- **Relationships:** Price's initial dismissiveness (*"What the hell kind of name is 'Soap', eh? How'd a muppet like you pass selection?"*) sets up their mentor-protégé arc.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Pick up sidearm; shoot targets from hip and ADS; knife watermelon; run the mock cargo ship CQB assault course under 60 seconds (target time: under 20 seconds for veteran).
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** First-person tutorial; ADS calibration; weapon switching speed; wall-penetration basics; sprint and mantle mechanics; dynamic difficulty recommendation based on course completion time.
- **Narrative Design:** Seamless diegetic tutorial: mechanics are justified through military training rather than abstract menu popups.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Catwalk view of Captain Price leaning over the railing; stopwatch timer; pop-up plywood terrorists and civilian targets.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Reverberant metal hangar acoustics; sharp report of suppressed MP5; ticking stopwatch; iconic watermelon slash audio.
- **Emotional Experience:** Mild performance anxiety, instructional focus, immediate drive to prove competence.
- **Political Significance:** Institutionalizes the British SAS as an elite, meritocratic brotherhood operating beyond standard civilian accountability.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Rebuilt character models for Gaz and Price with individual wool threading on boonies; realistic ambient occlusion in hangar; updated knife animations; Easter egg trophy for shooting Makarov through wall.
- **Evidence:** Gaz: *"Soap, eh? Good luck, mate."* Price: *"It's an improvement, but it's not hard to improve on garbage."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Demonstrates how modern shooters discipline the player's somatic body, equating mechanical gaming skill with soldierly worth.

### MISSION 2: Crew Expendable
- **Act:** Prologue
- **Timeline:** Day 1 — 02:00
- **Location:** Bering Strait (Rough International Waters)
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain Price, Gaz, Sgt. Wallcroft, Pvt. Griffen
- **Mission Premise:** Board an unflagged cargo vessel suspected of transporting nuclear contraband to the Middle East.
- **Story Progression:** The team secures the ship, discovers a Russian nuclear warhead with Arabic shipping manifests, and escapes before the ship sinks after being torpedoed by Russian MiGs.
- **Character Development:** Soap participates in his first real black operation. Price shows absolute cold lethality, shooting sleeping crewmen.
- **Relationships:** Price physically catches Soap as the deck collapses into the ocean, establishing his loyalty to his men.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Fast-rope onto deck; clear bridge and quarters; secure cargo hold; extract nuclear manifest; escape sinking vessel.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Pitching deck physics; tight hallway CQB; flashbang usage; timed linear extraction sprint.
- **Narrative Design:** Rapid escalation from stealth boarding to frantic, vertical environmental escape.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Torrential rain and rolling ocean waves; dramatic slow-motion catwalk collapse; Sea Knight helicopter extraction.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Roar of storm waves, thunder, metallic groaning of buckling hull plates, muffled MP5 gunfire, alarm sirens.
- **Emotional Experience:** Claustrophobia, adrenaline, spatial disorientation during flooding, surge of relief upon extraction.
- **Political Significance:** Depicts state sovereignty being suspended in international waters; unflagged vessels as nodes of rogue proliferation.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Volumetric rain; water droplets on weapon lenses; real-time ocean wave physics with dynamic buoyancy; vastly improved lighting and steam effects.
- **Evidence:** Price: *"We're going deep and we're going silent."* / *"Soap! Grab my hand!"*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Examines how surgical special-forces intervention is framed as a necessary, clean pre-emptive strike against rogue weapons of mass destruction.

### MISSION 3: The Coup
- **Act:** Prologue
- **Timeline:** Day 1 — 14:00
- **Location:** Unnamed Middle Eastern Capital City
- **Playable Character:** President Yasir Al-Fulani
- **Supporting Cast:** Khaled Al-Asad, Imran Zakhaev, Viktor Zakhaev
- **Mission Premise:** Inhabiting the deposed president during his capture, transportation, and public execution.
- **Story Progression:** Al-Fulani is dragged through his crumbling city, witnessing public slaughter before Al-Asad executes him on live television.
- **Character Development:** Al-Asad is introduced as a ruthless, theatrical demagogue; Zakhaev is glimpsed as the shadowy foreign patron.
- **Relationships:** Pure perpetrator-victim dynamic between Al-Asad and Al-Fulani.
- **Tactical Objectives:** None (survive and observe).
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Complete removal of movement controls; locked camera yaw/pitch restricted to 180 degrees; zero weapon capability.
- **Narrative Design:** Pure non-interactive cinematic drama executed from an embodied first-person perspective.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Car ride through burning streets; dogs barking; execution firing squads; television studio arena; point-blank Desert Eagle execution.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Muffled heartbeat, bruised breathing, chaotic crowd chanting in Arabic, gunshots, propaganda broadcast booming from loudspeakers.
- **Emotional Experience:** Helplessness, sensory horror, claustrophobic dread, shock at point-blank execution.
- **Political Significance:** Parlak & Kaygusuz (2025): Denies the Middle Eastern subject political agency; presents the region as an inherently violent space requiring Western intervention.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Sharpened environmental destruction, detailed civilian character models being executed on streets, HDR sunlight blinding Al-Fulani's eyes.
- **Evidence:** Al-Asad: *"Today we banish the Western devils and their corrupt puppets!"*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Demonstrates the manipulation of player agency to generate emotional shock while advancing a neocolonial narrative of Middle Eastern barbarism.

### MISSION 4: Blackout
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 2 — 01:45
- **Location:** Caucasus Mountains, Russia
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain Price, Gaz, Sgt. Kamarov (Loyalists), Nikolai
- **Mission Premise:** Link up with Russian Loyalists to rescue captive SAS informant Nikolai before he is executed.
- **Story Progression:** Bravo Team assists Kamarov in clearing a village, cuts power to an interrogation cottage, and rescues Nikolai.
- **Character Development:** Shows Price's impatience with proxy delays; introduces Kamarov's pragmatic opportunism; establishes Nikolai's resilience.
- **Relationships:** Tense, transactional alliance between Price and Kamarov; mutual respect between Price and Nikolai.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Infiltrate village swamp; provide sniper support to Loyalists; cut power to cottage; rescue Nikolai.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Suppressed weapons; thermal/night-vision goggles; coordinated sniper timing; power cut resulting in dynamic darkness.
- **Narrative Design:** Contrast between open valley sniper support and claustrophobic interior room clearing.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Helicopter searchlights scanning swamps; cutting circuit breaker plunging building into pitch black; flashlight sweeps.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Crickets, night wind, suppressed weapon chuffs, sudden frantic shouts of surprised guards, thunderous Loyalist artillery.
- **Emotional Experience:** Stealth tension, frustration with allied delays, calculated lethal satisfaction in darkness.
- **Political Significance:** Highlights the messy ethics of proxy warfare: Western forces trading tactical favors with local factions to secure intelligence assets.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Spectacular dynamic night-vision rendering; realistic green phosphor noise; volumetric fog in the marshlands.
- **Evidence:** Price: *"Kamarov, where's the informant?"* Kamarov: *"Patience, Captain, we must secure our flank."* Gaz: *"Not funny, Kamarov."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Analyzes how night-vision technology constructs asymmetric lethality, transforming combat into a mechanized slaughter of blinded adversaries.

### MISSION 5: Charlie Don't Surf
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 2 — 13:45
- **Location:** Middle Eastern Coastal Town
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. Paul Jackson (USMC)
- **Supporting Cast:** Lt. Vasquez, SSgt. Griggs, USMC 1st Recon
- **Mission Premise:** Marine airborne assault to storm the state television station and capture Khaled Al-Asad.
- **Story Progression:** Marines breach the coastal town and storm the broadcast center, only to find Al-Asad's speech is a pre-recorded loop.
- **Character Development:** Establishes Jackson as a dependable Marine rifleman, Vasquez as a vocal, aggressive commander, and Griggs as a heavy-weapons specialist.
- **Relationships:** USMC unit camaraderie; unyielding confidence in American military technological supremacy.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Fast-rope from Sea Knight; clear coastal warehouses; advance through urban streets; breach TV studio; secure broadcast room.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Urban street fighting; suppressing fire; corner-peeking; room breaching; clearing building basements.
- **Narrative Design:** Pacing builds toward an expected confrontation with Al-Asad, subverted by an empty control room and looping magnetic tape.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Low-flying helicopter armada skimming coastal waters; kicking down studio doors; rows of television monitors playing Al-Asad's face.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Blaring Arabic propaganda from television speakers, roaring helicopter rotors, deafening M16A4 and SAW gunfire, shouted Marine orders.
- **Emotional Experience:** Aggressive empowerment, sensory overload, anticlimax upon finding the empty broadcast studio.
- **Political Significance:** Evokes the US invasion of Iraq (2003); title references *Apocalypse Now*, signaling American imperial hubris in chasing phantom targets.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Completely re-textured Arabic signage; realistic dust motes dancing in studio lighting; enhanced facial sweat and grit on Marine models.
- **Evidence:** Vasquez: *"Keep moving! Secure the studio!"* / *"It's a recording. The bastard isn't here."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Deconstructs the procedural reproduction of War on Terror invasion aesthetics and the futility of chasing mediated villainy.

### MISSION 6: The Bog
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 2 — 22:05
- **Location:** Middle Eastern Capital City
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. Paul Jackson
- **Supporting Cast:** Lt. Vasquez, SSgt. Griggs, "War Pig" M1 Abrams Tank Crew
- **Mission Premise:** Night urban push to rescue an immobilized M1 Abrams tank stranded in a hostile tenement district.
- **Story Progression:** Jackson's squad fights through an apartment complex, establishes a defensive perimeter around the tank, and uses laser designators to direct airstrikes.
- **Character Development:** Demonstrates the intense mutual reliance between infantry and heavy armor in urban environments.
- **Relationships:** Vasquez's fierce dedication to protecting the tank crew; Jackson's role as the forward combat leader.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Advance through tenement slums; reach stranded tank; eliminate RPG teams on overpasses; plant IR beacon for close air support.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Night combat; night-vision usage; IR beacon placement; laser designator targeting Cobra helicopter airstrikes; heavy enemy suppression.
- **Narrative Design:** High-intensity defensive stand; transitions from offensive room clearing to frantic perimeter defense under 360-degree attack.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Tracers cutting through dark night sky; fiery explosions of RPG impacts; Cobra gunships screaming overhead unleashing Hellfire missiles.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Constant radio calls for fire support; explosive crump of RPGs hitting concrete; deafening 20mm rotary cannon strafing runs.
- **Emotional Experience:** Severe pressure, sensory panic, exhilaration when close air support obliterates enemy positions.
- **Political Significance:** Depicts urban combat as an asymmetric meat-grinder; local urban geography framed as a chaotic swamp of hostile combatants.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Modernized thermal optics on Cobra strikes; HDR night lighting with blinding muzzle flashes illuminating tenement walls.
- **Evidence:** Tank Commander: *"Thanks for the assist, Marines! We're dead in the water until morning!"*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Examines how the game aestheticizes close air support, converting catastrophic urban destruction into a visual reward for mechanical compliance.

### MISSION 7: Hunted
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 2 — 03:02
- **Location:** Western Russia (Rural Farmland)
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. John "Soap" MacTavish
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain Price, Gaz, Nikolai
- **Mission Premise:** Bravo Team must evade Ultranationalist tracking patrols through farmland after their helicopter is shot down.
- **Story Progression:** The team escorts the unarmed Nikolai through barns and fields, downs an attack helicopter with a Stinger missile, and reaches the AC-130 extraction zone.
- **Character Development:** Highlights the SAS squad’s survival skills when stripped of air superiority and heavy support.
- **Relationships:** Price and Gaz protecting Nikolai; Soap trusted to operate heavy anti-aircraft weaponry.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Evade enemy search patrols; bypass guard dogs; acquire Stinger missile; destroy Mi-24 Hind gunship; reach AC-130 extraction junkyard.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Stealth evasion in grass; handling guard dog attacks (melee neck-snap mechanic); surface-to-air missile locking and firing.
- **Narrative Design:** Fluid transitions between tense evasion, panic when spotted by helicopters, and explosive retaliation.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Enemy helicopter searchlight sweeping through barn floorboards; barking attack dogs leaping through mist; Stinger missile contrail.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Growling hounds, distant Russian radio orders, buzzing helicopter turbines, wind whistling through cornfields.
- **Emotional Experience:** Extreme vulnerability, fear of detection, cathartic triumph upon downing the pursuing gunship.
- **Political Significance:** Establishes the Ultranationalists as a well-equipped, ruthless paramilitary force capable of matching Western technology on their home terrain.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Realistic foliage density; dynamic light shafts piercing through wooden barn slats; authentic fur and teeth rendering on guard dogs.
- **Evidence:** Price: *"They're hunting us with dogs. Stay low and keep quiet."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Explores the procedural rhetoric of tactical vulnerability, demonstrating that survival hinges on recognizing the limits of individual firepower.

### MISSION 8: Death From Above
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 2 — 04:20
- **Location:** Airspace over Western Russia
- **Playable Character:** AC-130 TV Operator (USAF 4th SOS)
- **Supporting Cast:** AC-130 Fire Control Officer, Pilot, Bravo Team (as flashing infrared IR strobes on ground)
- **Mission Premise:** Provide aerial thermal fire support to Bravo Team and Nikolai as they navigate rural highways toward extraction.
- **Story Progression:** The gunship decimates Ultranationalist infantry, technicals, and armored vehicles, securing Bravo Team’s escape.
- **Character Development:** The aircrew exhibit complete emotional detachment, viewing combat as an abstract video game of thermal signatures.
- **Relationships:** Total technological hierarchy: the aircrew act as omnipotent guardian angels over the vulnerable, running dots of Bravo Team.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Identify flashing friendly IR strobes; eliminate enemy vehicles and foot soldiers; protect church and water tower; clear junkyard.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Top-down thermal camera view; switching between 25mm Gatling gun, 40mm Bofors cannon, and 105mm Howitzer; lead-target calculation; civilian vehicle fire restriction.
- **Narrative Design:** Radical aesthetic and mechanical rupture: completely divorces the player from ground-level physical danger.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Grainy black-and-white CRT scanlines; camera vibration upon 105mm discharge; screen-filling thermal blooms from exploding fuel trucks.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Muffled, distant bass thuds of heavy shells; whine of aircraft electronics; chillingly calm, casual crew banter.
- **Emotional Experience:** God-like power, clinical detachment, disturbing amusement at violent destruction, moral disquiet.
- **Political Significance:** Der Derian’s (2009) "Virtuous War" manifested: lethal imperial violence sanitized of blood, agony, and physical consequence.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Enhanced thermal CRT artifacting, realistic heat dissipation on vehicle engines, authentic FLIR bloom shaders.
- **Evidence:** FCO: *"Kaboom."* / *"See that guy by the car? Smoke him."* / *"You are cleared hot, acknowledge."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** The pinnacle of militainment critique: the game literally converts real-world aerial slaughter into an actual video game within a video game.

### MISSION 10: Shock and Awe
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 3 — 18:30
- **Location:** Middle Eastern Capital City
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. Paul Jackson
- **Supporting Cast:** Lt. Vasquez, SSgt. Griggs, Capt. Pelayo (Downed Pilot)
- **Mission Premise:** Final American air assault into the capital to capture Al-Asad; sudden race to rescue a downed Cobra pilot before a nuclear detonation.
- **Story Progression:** The Marines push deep into the city; an escort Cobra is shot down; Vasquez orders a rescue; Jackson pulls Pelayo from the wreck; the helicopter escapes just as an underground nuclear warhead detonates, vaporizing the city and downing the transport.
- **Character Development:** Vasquez demonstrates noble Marine loyalty ("no one left behind"); Jackson executes selfless heroism, which ironically leads to his demise.
- **Relationships:** Total unit cohesion in rescuing a female comrade; shared doomed fate.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Fire minigun from helicopter; support ground troops; land in hostile hot zone; carry Capt. Pelayo to transport; evacuate capital.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** On-rails helicopter minigun shooting; timed 90-second rescue sprint under intense fire; carrying incapacitated NPC back to ramp.
- **Narrative Design:** Brilliant tonal reversal: moves from triumphant air cavalry spectacle to frantic rescue, followed by total apocalyptic annihilation.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Helicopter spinning out of control; blinding nuclear flash washing out the screen; shockwave flipping vehicles and tearing the Sea Knight out of the sky.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Minigun whine, alarms screaming, frantic radio dispatch warning of nuclear arming, sudden blinding silence of the atomic detonation followed by a roaring shockwave.
- **Emotional Experience:** Exhilaration, heroic urgency, sudden dread, absolute shock and disbelief at narrative catastrophe.
- **Political Significance:** Subverts post-9/11 War on Terror confidence: demonstrates that conventional imperial power is completely vulnerable to asymmetrical weapons of mass destruction.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Volumetric nuclear cloud physics; blinding HDR exposure; breathtakingly realistic shockwave tearing apart skyscrapers and palm trees in real time.
- **Evidence:** Central Command: *"All forces, pull out immediately! NEST teams confirm a nuclear device is arming!"* Vasquez: *"We're not leaving her!"*
- **Critical Synthesis:** A landmark in video game storytelling that permanently shattered the trope of the invincible American military protagonist.

### MISSION 11: Aftermath
- **Act:** Act I
- **Timeline:** Day 3 — Post-Detonation (Immediate)
- **Location:** Ground Zero, Middle Eastern Capital
- **Playable Character:** Sgt. Paul Jackson (Mortally Wounded)
- **Supporting Cast:** Dead squadmates, dying US Marines
- **Mission Premise:** Inhabiting the dying body of Sergeant Paul Jackson crawling through the radioactive epicenter of the nuclear detonation.
- **Story Progression:** Jackson awakens inside the crashed helicopter, crawls outside, witnesses the destruction of the city, and dies.
- **Character Development:** Jackson's complete bodily disintegration; the ultimate anti-heroic demise.
- **Relationships:** Total isolation; surrounded by the corpses of Vasquez and Pelayo.
- **Tactical Objectives:** None (crawl forward).
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** All weapons, HUD, sprint, and jump mechanics disabled; agonizingly slow crawl locomotion; forced collapse and flatline.
- **Narrative Design:** Pure experiential tragedy; forces the player to endure the physical consequence of war without ludic distraction.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Towering nuclear mushroom cloud; burning playground swings; falling radioactive ash; blinding white sun.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Absolute absence of music; heavy, rattling, choking breaths; irregular, failing heartbeat monitor; crackle of burning rubble.
- **Emotional Experience:** Grief, helplessness, existential horror, profound vulnerability.
- **Political Significance:** Direct visual depiction of nuclear devastation; strips military intervention of all glory and romance.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Terrifyingly realistic volumetric ash particles; visceral skin burns and torn uniforms on dead Marines; haunting radiation glare.
- **Evidence:** Radio Dispatch (Distorted): *"Mayday, Mayday... any US forces, acknowledge... is anybody alive out there?..."* [Heartbeat flatlines].
- **Critical Synthesis:** The ultimate antithesis to militainment: transforms the player from a consumer of violent spectacle into a dying victim of nuclear fallout.

### MISSION 13: All Ghillied Up
- **Act:** Act II
- **Timeline:** 15 Years Ago (1996)
- **Location:** Pripyat, Ukraine (Chernobyl Exclusion Zone)
- **Playable Character:** Lt. John Price
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain MacMillan
- **Mission Premise:** Flashback to 1996: Two-man SAS black-ops infiltration of Pripyat to assassinate rogue arms dealer Imran Zakhaev.
- **Story Progression:** Price and MacMillan navigate through radioactive ruins, evading massive Ultranationalist patrols and armored convoys.
- **Character Development:** Establishes Price's foundational mentorship under MacMillan; reveals his origins as a patient, disciplined sniper.
- **Relationships:** Absolute tactical deference from young Price to the master sniper MacMillan.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Follow MacMillan; maintain stealth in ghillie suits; synchronize sniper shots; crawl beneath enemy armor convoy; reach Hotel Polissya.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** Spatial stealth based on posture and movement velocity; ghillie camouflage meter; synchronized double-taps; avoiding wild dogs and radiation zones.
- **Narrative Design:** Masterclass in spatial tension; pacing built entirely around waiting, crawling, and holding breath.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Walking under the treads of BMP armored vehicles; overgrown Soviet playgrounds; decaying brutalist architecture of Pripyat.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Geiger counter ticking; eerie wind blowing through abandoned apartment blocks; MacMillan's whispered Scottish brogue.
- **Emotional Experience:** Suffocating tension, paranoia, awe at the haunting post-apocalyptic environment.
- **Political Significance:** Bos (2018): Commercialized dark tourism of Chernobyl; frames the post-Soviet space as a ruined, lawless frontier of nuclear smuggling.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Unbelievable foliage overhaul; individual blades of grass reacting to wind and player crawling; photorealistic recreation of Pripyat landmarks.
- **Evidence:** MacMillan: *"Stay low. Don't make any sudden moves. An entire company is moving through."* / *"Look at this place... fifty thousand people used to live here. Now it's a ghost town."*
- **Critical Synthesis:** The pinnacle of procedural stealth: proves that silence and complete physical constraint can generate greater interactive tension than violent spectacle.

### MISSION 14: One Shot, One Kill
- **Act:** Act II
- **Timeline:** 15 Years Ago (1996)
- **Location:** Pripyat, Ukraine
- **Playable Character:** Lt. John Price
- **Supporting Cast:** Captain MacMillan, Imran Zakhaev
- **Mission Premise:** Take the long-range sniper shot on Imran Zakhaev from Hotel Polissya and escape through the city with an injured MacMillan.
- **Story Progression:** Price severs Zakhaev’s arm with a .50-cal sniper shot; an enemy helicopter crashes into MacMillan during escape; Price carries MacMillan to the Pripyat amusement park and holds out until extraction.
- **Character Development:** Price proves his moral character by carrying his crippled commander rather than abandoning him.
- **Relationships:** Complete inversion of power: the mentor becomes helpless, relying entirely on his protégé for physical survival.
- **Tactical Objectives:** Adjust for wind/distance and shoot Zakhaev; rappel from hotel; carry MacMillan; place Claymores at Ferris wheel; defend LZ.
- **Gameplay Mechanics:** High-caliber sniping with Coriolis effect; carrying an incapacitated NPC (forcing pistol-only combat); defensive Claymore perimeter setup.
- **Narrative Design:** High-stakes transition from calculated assassination to desperate, frantic defense under overwhelming odds.
- **Cinematic Elements:** Zakhaev’s arm flying off in slow motion; Mi-28 Havoc spinning out of control and crushing MacMillan; Ferris wheel looming over defensive stand.
- **Sound & Atmosphere:** Thunderous boom of Barrett .50 cal; alarm klaxons; shrieking metal of falling helicopter; frantic calls for Allied extraction.
- **Emotional Experience:** Precision triumph followed by immediate panic, physical exhaustion, desperation at the Ferris wheel stand.
- **Political Significance:** Illustrates the unintended consequences of targeted assassination: the failure to fully kill Zakhaev birthed the modern 2011 crisis.
- **Remaster Comparison:** Vastly improved sniper scope optics; physical bullet physics rendering; enhanced lighting illuminating the iconic Pripyat yellow Ferris wheel.
- **Evidence:** MacMillan: *"You took his arm off! He's in shock, but he's not dead! Move, we've gotta go!"*
- **Critical Synthesis:** Deconstructs the myth of the clean assassination: surgical strikes frequently produce catastrophic collateral failures that ripple across generations.

### MISSION 15: Heat
- **Act:** Act II
- **Timeline:** Day 4 — 09:40
- **Location:** Northern Azerbaijan (Mountain Village)
